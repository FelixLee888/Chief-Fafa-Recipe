Red wine amber sugar

Made with red wine, cold weather and sugar, refrigerated to solidify, cut into pieces, and then dried at low temperature until a dry, matte sugar shell forms on the surface. It has a rich, floral aroma and is less sweet than traditional amber sugar.

Ingredients

  • Red wine 200g
  • water 100g
  • Sugar 440g
  • Hantian 5g

Instructions

  1. Take 50 g of red wine, add water and cold water, and bring to a boil.
  2. Add sugar and cook until the sugar is completely dissolved; dip a chopstick into the syrup and cook until it becomes stringy.
  3. Turn off the heat, pour in the remaining 150 g of red wine, stir well and skim off the foam.
  4. Line the mold with baking paper, pour in the sugar liquid, and refrigerate until solid.
  5. Unmold and cut into small pieces.
  6. Cover a wire rack with baking paper to prevent sticking, put it into a fruit dryer, and dry at 35-40°C for 3-4 days; adjust the time according to the size of the sugar cubes and crystallization speed.
  7. After crystals form on the surface, turn over and continue drying until the entire crystal is complete.

Notes

  • Successful amber sugar will have a dry, matte surface.
  • If the surface is still shiny or sticky, it means you need to continue drying.
  • The drying temperature is not recommended to exceed 45°C, otherwise it may cause the surface to dry quickly, the internal moisture to be difficult to escape, or even crack or melt.
  • This recipe has reduced sugar, and if the amount of sugar is reduced further, it may not crystallize.
  • Be sure to line the wire rack with baking paper, otherwise the sugar cubes may stick to the wire rack.
